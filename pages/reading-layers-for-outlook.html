03 — Human-Centred Concepts

Reading Layers for Outlook

A speculative concept for Outlook's composer: a calm, monochrome way to show what matters in an email — without adding more bold, colour, or clutter.

Role: Product Designer Year: 2026 Type: Independent Concept

Observation

Writing a long email, I kept reaching for bold, italics, underline, bullet points, and extra spacing to explain what mattered most. The more I used, the noisier the email got — and the harder it became to tell what actually needed attention versus what was just there for context.

This is a small, independent product-design concept exploring that one moment — not a shipped Microsoft feature, and not based on formal user research.

The Problem

Outlook gives writers plenty of formatting tools, but no shared sense of hierarchy. So everyone invents their own: some bold the deadline, some underline it too, some add a bracketed aside in italics "just in case." Stack enough of these decisions in one email and every sentence starts competing for the same amount of attention.

The reader has to decode formatting choices instead of just reading the message — and the writer has no simple way to say "this matters more" or "this is just context" without reaching for more emphasis.

Design Question

How might Outlook help writers create clear visual hierarchy in an email without filling it with more competing formatting?

The Concept

Reading Layers adds one optional hierarchy system alongside Outlook's existing formatting: three tonal levels a writer can assign to any selection, based on how important it is — not how it should look.

Primary The main message, action, or deadline. Highest contrast, unmissable.
Reply needed by Friday.
Standard The main body of the email. The default, readable tone.
Here's the plan for next week.
Supporting Context, asides, references — present, but clearly secondary.
(Optional: see attached notes.)

Bold, italics, underline, and bullet points remain exactly where they are. Reading Layers sits alongside them as a hierarchy system, not a replacement.

Interaction Model

1

Just type the email

No selecting, no tagging as you go. Write the way you always do — bold, bullets, and all — with zero extra steps while composing.

Hi team, quick update on the launch… confirm staging is locked by 8/5
2

One click: Auto-balance

When it's ready, Outlook reads the whole message and assigns Primary, Standard, and Supporting automatically — no line-by-line work required.

Balanced — updated 2 lines for a calmer hierarchy. ✕
3

Preview, never surprise

Light, dark, and an accessible fallback view are one tap away, so the hierarchy can be checked the way a recipient will actually see it.

Accessibility

Contrast is never sacrificed

Supporting text stays within accessible contrast ratios. Hierarchy comes from size, weight, spacing, and a subtle side marker — not from making text harder to read.

Colour is never the only signal

A left-edge marker on Supporting text, plus a wider background-fill and font-size spread in the accessible view, mean the hierarchy still reads for anyone who can't rely on colour or subtle contrast.

Falls back cleanly

Recipients on other clients see standard formatted text — bold, italics, bullets — with no broken layout. Reading Layers is a sender-side view, not a dependency.

Try assigning hierarchy to a real email

Type as normal, then click "Auto-balance" to assign the hierarchy in one go. Select any line to override it, preview in Light, Dark, or Accessible, or reset at any time.

Reflection

This isn't about making email more decorative — it's about giving writers one calm way to say "this matters more" without stacking on more bold, more colour, or more bullets to get there.

Reading Layers is a speculative, independent concept. It doesn't reflect a real Outlook roadmap, and no formal research informed it — just a repeated, small frustration worth designing around.
