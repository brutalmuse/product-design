03 — Human-Centred Concepts

ThreadSpace

A memory-based way to organize scattered email discussions. ThreadSpace is a concept feature for Outlook that helps users collect related emails into one persistent discussion space.

Role: Product Designer Year: 2026 Type: Concept UX
Brand identity
ThreadSpace

Issue Mapping

Outlook search works well when users remember the exact keyword, sender, or subject line. But in real work situations, people often remember conversations through context — a project, decision, meeting, client, or task — rather than a precise search term.

When related emails appear scattered across dates, senders, and threads, users are forced to manually scan results and rebuild the discussion themselves. This creates friction, repeated searching, and a higher chance of losing important context.

Design Opportunity

How might Outlook help users collect scattered but related emails into one persistent discussion space, so they can return to the full context without searching again? Outlook is not just where emails arrive — it is where work gets remembered.

The real Airbnb and Webflow offers are scattered between unrelated promotional emails — no grouping, no way to tell which result is the one that matters.

Design Principles

1

Reduce search dependency

Move beyond keyword-based search by helping users group related emails into one discussion space.

2

Preserve discussion context

Keep scattered emails connected so users can return to the full conversation without rebuilding it manually.

3

Make grouping feel familiar

Use drag-and-drop folder behaviour to make organizing discussions feel natural and low effort.

Initial Direction

Selected emails are dragged into ThreadSpace, turning scattered search results into one organized discussion folder. This reduces repeated search and helps users rebuild context faster — designed for users who remember context, not exact keywords.

Emails are selected in the Inbox, added to a ThreadSpace, and the folder above the list brings the grouped discussion back. The walkthrough runs on its own — click anywhere in it to take over.
