AI Interaction Study

A study of how buttons should behave, not just how they should look — twelve personalities, each built from a single word.

Role: Product Designer Year: 2026 Type: AI Interaction Study

Every button here starts from a feeling, not a style.

Instead of designing a button by visual style, each one here starts from a word — hesitate, commit, urgent, undo — and asks Claude to interpret that as an interaction. The same loop runs for all twelve: a one-line prompt, an AI-generated interpretation, a critique of what felt off, a refinement prompt with real constraints, then a final button.

The output is a small library of buttons with unnecessarily specific personalities — one that dodges the cursor once before letting you click, one that resists and demands a second, deliberate confirmation, one that only becomes visible as you approach it. Every button still just says "Press."

Findings

The clearest finding: behaviour is a design material in its own right, not a finishing touch applied after the visuals. A hesitation, a resistance, a slow compression — each reads as intent faster than a label or an icon does, and each needed the same care as any other detail: timing, easing, displacement, all tuned until the feeling landed.

Starting from a feeling rather than a visual reference also proved to be a fast, disciplined way to explore interaction design at scale — twelve distinct personalities in the time it would normally take to explore one. The same approach holds beyond buttons: for transitions, empty states, loading, any moment where an interface needs to feel like something before it says anything.
