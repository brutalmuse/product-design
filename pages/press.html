AI Interaction Study

PRESS.

An AI-directed study of how buttons should behave, not just how they should look — twelve behaviours interpreted from a single word each.

The premise

A single button, twelve words. Each word — hesitate, commit, urgent, undo, danger, celebrate, wait, quiet, heavy, fragile, curious, done — was handed to an AI as the only instruction, with no visual spec attached. The goal was to see what a model infers about motion, timing, and feedback purely from the emotional shape of a word.

What came out of it

The AI consistently reached for motion as the primary vocabulary — dodging, jittering, pulsing, freezing — before it reached for color. Words with an implied consequence ("danger," "commit") produced confirmation states; words with an implied absence ("quiet," "wait") produced restraint. The exercise reframed the button not as a static object but as a short performance triggered by a single word.
